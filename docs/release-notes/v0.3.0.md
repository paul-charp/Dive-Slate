# Dive Slate 0.3.0 — first release

An Android app that turns a dive log into a **transparent slate**: a compact badge
of your profile to drop over a photo or a video frame.

Share a dive out of Subsurface-mobile, pick a palette, and save it to your gallery
or send it wherever you want it. About three taps end to end.

## Install

Download **`dive-slate-0.3.0.apk`** below and open it. **Android 10 or newer.**

It is not on the Play Store, so your phone will ask once whether to allow installs
from wherever you opened the file — a browser or a file manager. If you want to
check the download first, `dive-slate-0.3.0.apk.sha256` holds its checksum.

If you already have a build of this app that you compiled yourself, **uninstall it
first**. It was signed with the Android SDK's debug key, this one is properly
signed, and Android refuses to replace an app whose signature changed.

## What it does

- Reads **Subsurface** (`.ssrf`) and **UDDF** (`.uddf`) logs — shared in from
  Subsurface-mobile, opened from your files, or the bundled sample. Detection is
  by content, so a renamed log still works.
- Renders a profile silhouette, the site name, and a few big numbers. No axes and
  no legend: at a third of frame width on a phone those are noise, not
  information.
- Exports a **transparent PNG** to your gallery, or hands it to the share sheet.
- Nine palettes, wide or tall layout, and control over which figures appear.

The output has no background — that is the whole point, and why the preview sits
on a checkerboard. The opacity slider moves the scrim panel behind the text and
never the ink, so the contrast the palettes were built for survives whatever it
ends up sitting on.

Deco time is measured as the hang actually served — from reaching the ceiling on
the way up until the obligation clears — not the span over which deco was owed.
Figures round up: a 44.4 m dive is a 45 m dive.

## Updates

The app checks this page for a newer release once a day, and offers it to you when
there is one. The download is verified against the checksum published here before
Android is asked to install anything, and refused if it does not match. Those two
requests are the only thing the app does with the network.
